What We Do — Automation & Process Control

Power Monitoring & SEL RTAC Consulting

Turn protective relays and electrical equipment into a live, integrated power-monitoring system — SEL RTAC programming, operator HMIs, secure networking, and clean ties into your control system and historian, engineered by people who speak both relays and OT.

What We Deliver

  • SEL RTAC Programming & Automation — RTAC gateways configured to integrate SEL, GE, Basler, and other protective relays over Modbus TCP/Serial, DNP3, and other industrial protocols, with automated reporting, alarm and operational logic, and NTP/PTP time synchronization for accurate event recording.
  • SEL RTAC HMI Development — operator and engineering screens for relay status and measurements, electrical one-line diagrams, communication health, trending, and equipment alarms — plus local field troubleshooting interfaces for MCCs and electrical gear.
  • SEL Blueframe Implementation — disturbance monitoring and event analysis, protective-relay configuration management, relay password and access control, and automated configuration reporting.
  • Network Design & Architecture — power-monitoring network architectures, relay networking parameters, and network drawings covering fiber, switches, patch panels, and communication pathways.
  • Data Integration & Historian Connectivity — relay and RTAC data into Emerson DeltaV, Ignition, and Allen-Bradley PLCs; SQL-based reporting; and electrical data into enterprise historians like AVEVA PI System.
  • Reliability & Cybersecurity — backup strategies, RTAC firmware upgrades, secure remote engineering access, and network hardening from data diodes to credential management.

Relays Meet OT

Power monitoring lives at the seam between the electrical world and the control system — and that seam is where most projects stall. Relay engineers don't always speak OT networking, and integrators don't always speak protection. We do both: SEL RTAC and Blueframe on one side, DeltaV, Ignition, PLCs, historians, and ISA/IEC 62443-graded networks on the other.

From Relay to Reporting

We take you the whole way — relay communications and time sync, the RTAC logic that turns raw data into events and alarms, the HMI your operators actually watch, and the integration that lands electrical data in the same systems your process runs on. One team, no handoffs between the electrical and automation scopes.

What We Build

A power-monitoring system is more than a screen — it's relay communications, RTAC logic, networking, integration, and the documentation that holds it together. These are the pieces we engineer and tie into one working whole.

RTAC & Relay Integration

SEL RTAC gateways tied to SEL, GE, Basler, and other relays over Modbus TCP/Serial and DNP3. Relay data processing, alarm generation, and operational logic. NTP/PTP time synchronization for accurate event recording and disturbance analysis.

HMI & Visualization

Operator and engineering HMIs for relay status, one-line diagrams, communication health, trending, and equipment alarms. Local troubleshooting interfaces at MCCs and electrical equipment, built for the people working the gear.

Network & Architecture

Power-monitoring network design and documentation — IP addressing, protocols, and segmentation. Drawings for fiber infrastructure, Ethernet switches, patch panels, and communication pathways, so the network is a map, not a mystery.

Data, History & Reporting

Integration into DeltaV, Ignition, and Allen-Bradley platforms. Local storage with SQL-based reporting and electrical data flowing into enterprise historians like AVEVA PI. Automated reports for fault events, comms health, and system status.

Reliability & Security

Backups for RTAC programs, relay settings, and configurations. Firmware upgrades and system maintenance. Secure remote engineering access, data diodes and isolation, Active Directory integration, SDN, and relay credential management.

Troubleshooting & Validation

Diagnose and resolve relay connectivity, serial communication, and Ethernet/fiber issues across switches and gateways. Communication loop checks and system validation testing to prove the data path end to end.
